Part 1 — Why one identity layer changes everything

The original CDP PRD treated "customer" as one account. In practice, TRR has learned that one real person often shows up as multiple accounts — a personal email, a business email, an anonymous mobile session — and every team downstream was quietly building its own guess at who these belonged to.

The fix is not a new team-specific tool. It is a shared identity layer — a Person ID — that the CDP, the Rep App, and the Marketing platform all key off. Get this right once, and three products stop disagreeing with each other.

Part 2 — CDP: the decision engine (recap)

CDP

Unchanged in purpose: given everything known about a person right now, decide what should happen, and be able to explain why. Identity, signals, model outputs, eligibility rules, and explainability — see the prototype's CDP screens for the full detail. What changes is the key: every screen now reads and writes against person_id, not a single account ID.

Part 3 — Identity Layer: Customer 360 Identity API

IDENTITY

Problem

TRR stores multiple identities for the same person — different emails, phones, user IDs, devices — with no consistent way to merge or surface them. Reps see fragments. Marketing sends duplicates. Models miscalculate LTV, AOV, and churn because they are scored per-account instead of per-person.

What it does

  • Accepts any identifier (TRR user ID, email, phone, or in future a device ID)
  • Returns a unified Person ID plus the full graph of linked identities
  • Sources merges from Segment's Identity Resolution, synced into TRR on a scheduled job
GET /identity/lookup?identifier=jordyn@resellerco.com { "person_id": "PER-4471", "identities": { "trr_ids": ["88213", "90441"], "emails": ["jordyn@gmail.com", "jordyn@resellerco.com"], "phones": ["+1-222-333-4444"] }, "match_confidence": 0.94, "source": "Segment", "last_updated": "2026-07-14T09:14:00Z" }

Open risks, called out explicitly

  • Mismatches: Segment can incorrectly merge two people who share a device or email alias. Match confidence is surfaced everywhere identity is shown, not hidden as a backend detail.
  • Un-merging: Segment can later split a profile it previously merged. v1 re-syncs nightly and flags recently-split people; it does not retroactively correct historical reports.
  • Privacy: merging a personal and a business account raises real data-boundary questions — e.g. a rep should not see private personal purchase history bleeding into a business context without cause. This needs a privacy/legal review before broad rollout, not just an engineering sign-off.

Part 4 — Rep App

REP APP

Problem

A rep who searches one email only sees one account — missing the rest of the relationship. That means slower case handling, duplicate outreach, and reps making decisions on incomplete information.

User stories

As a rep, I want to search using any identifier — email, phone, or TRR ID — so I can find the customer regardless of which account they used.
As a rep, I want to see every linked account for a person in one place, so I understand their full relationship with TRR before I engage.
As a rep, I want person-level totals (lifetime spend, orders, consignments), so I can prioritize and personalize the conversation.
As a rep, I want a clear message when identity cannot be confidently resolved, so I know to treat two accounts separately rather than guess.
As a rep, I want to log an interaction against a person, so future reps and models see it as new signal, not just a note.

Non-goals for v1

No case management / ticketing (existing CX tool owns this) No automatic model retraining from rep feedback in v1

Part 5 — Marketing Intelligence Platform

MARKETING

Problem

Marketing today builds segments inside each channel tool separately (ESP, ad platforms, SMS), which means logic drifts, suppression is inconsistent, and nobody can say for certain that a "high-value" audience in email matches the one in paid social.

What it does

  • Audience Builder — combine CDP signals, model scores, and consent flags into a rule (e.g. propensity > 0.70 AND consented AND NOT suppressed), with a live count as the rule changes
  • Audience Library — every saved audience, its size, its criteria, and where it is synced
  • Channel Sync — push a saved audience to one or more channels (email/ESP, Meta Ads, Google Ads, SMS), with per-channel match rates and sync history

Why this is different from segments built inside a channel tool

Because audiences are built directly on CDP signals and the identity layer, they are person-level (deduplicated across linked accounts) and consent-aware by construction — an audience cannot include someone the CDP has already suppressed, and it does not need separate logic per channel to stay that way.

Non-goals for v1

No campaign creative or email building No A/B testing or attribution modeling (Phase 2+) No real-time (sub-hour) sync in v1

Part 6 — Revised phasing

PHASE 1
Foundation
  • CDP: identity, signals, model outputs (read-only)
  • Identity API: lookup + linked accounts
PHASE 2
Decisioning & first surfaces
  • CDP: eligibility rules, priority logic
  • Rep App: search + unified profile
  • Marketing: Audience Builder
PHASE 3
Scale & trust
  • CDP: explainability, decision logs
  • Rep App: escalation handling, interaction logging
  • Marketing: multi-channel sync, sync monitoring
Rep App: ↓30% duplicate lookups, ↓20% case handling time
Marketing: ↓ duplicate sends, ↑ targeting precision
DS: ↑ model accuracy from person-level features
Platform: % of teams reading from CDP vs. building their own logic

Each phase still stands alone and ships value on its own — the identity layer just means Phase 2's Rep App and Marketing work do not have to solve identity resolution themselves, because Phase 1 already did.
